# PART 2 — THE DECLINE-FLOW LOOP

**Still the distinctive move.** No changes since the 90-day plan. Deepened here with compliance detail.

**2.1 In plain terms, how it works:**

1. An SBA banker, commercial bank business banker, CDFI loan officer, or online lender partnerships contact reviews an applicant and declines them.
2. The banker uses the Handoff Toolkit — a script and a single-page intake form co-branded with the bank — to introduce the declined applicant to Burkham.
3. The applicant, if interested, fills out the Decline-Flow Handoff Intake form (lives in FunnelForge, routes to Console's Consent & Authorization Center module 1.5).
4. Applicant pays for the Funding Readiness Score™ Blueprint ($497 or $997) — the diagnostic that assesses the 8 components.
5. Blueprint output routes them to Buildout, Placement, or refer-out. Buildout is the most common decline-flow outcome (they're not-yet-fundable but curable in 30–90 days).
6. Buildout + Placement over 6–18 months. Applicant becomes re-bankable.
7. Applicant returns to originating bank ready-to-approve. Quarterly aggregate report to the banker documenting the returnable-client outcomes. Optional per-applicant update if applicant opts in.

**2.2 Who does the declining:**

- SBA preferred lenders (top 25 on Dream 100)
- Commercial bank business banking officers (top 15 regional banks on Dream 100)
- CDFI loan officers (top 10)
- Online lender partnerships (top 10 — OnDeck, Bluevine, Fundbox, Funding Circle, Kabbage, Credibly, Rapid Finance, Lendio decline routing, Nav, SmartBiz)

Also, occasionally: a credit union declining a member business, or a bank's SBA group referring within-bank to Burkham (rare in V1; more likely V1.5).

**2.3 Why would they send us someone they just declined:**

The clean answer, and the entire value exchange: **because they get a returnable client back in 6–18 months, and because it improves their applicant experience without regulatory exposure.**

The pieces:
- The banker gets to give the applicant an operational answer ("here's a firm to help you get ready") instead of a dead-end ("come back next year").
- The bank's decline NPS improves (this matters to modern commercial banking metrics).
- The bank gets access to the quarterly Capital Access Briefing intelligence.
- The bank keeps the client. When the client re-emerges in 6–18 months, they come back to *that bank*, not to a competitor.

**What is NOT the reason:** revenue share. There is none. This is a critical structural choice — see 2.5.

**2.4 What they get back:**

- **Returnable client** — the primary economic value. The client comes back to the originating bank, re-bankable, in 6–18 months.
- **Quarterly aggregate report** — anonymized: declines received from your institution / diagnostics run / returnable-client returns / capital placed elsewhere in the interim / any wrong-fit decline-to-engage. Written by Compliance & Evidence Department.
- **Optional per-applicant update** — only if applicant opts in through Consent & Authorization Center.
- **Quarterly Capital Access Briefing** — banker distribution list access. Also the Decline-Flow Banker-Side Briefing (8-page tailored version) quarterly.
- **Invitation to the quarterly briefing webinar** — private, invitation-only Zoom, Ivan presents live.
- **Goodwill and applicant-experience improvement** — real, measurable, boring, and correct.

**2.5 If there's a fee: how much, when paid:**

**There is no fee. Zero. No revenue share, no referral fee, no per-lead fee, no back-end fee, no consulting fee, no per-diligence fee, no per-close fee.**

**Rationale — this is the load-bearing structural choice:**

Referral compensation from a firm placing capital to a bank generating decline-flow referrals is a regulatory landmine. RESPA (for real-estate-touching finance), anti-tying rules under the Bank Holding Company Act, anti-kickback provisions across banking regulation, and state-level consumer financial protection laws all constrain payments in referral relationships involving financial services. Even where technically permissible, disclosure obligations and reputational risk make it not worth the operational simplicity.

The clean answer is no fee. This makes the partnership defensible to any regulator, at any level, in any state, without exception.

**Second-order flag:** cross-venture referrals (Group D) are internally-priced through arm's-length intercompany services agreement (Pack Section 7), NOT through referral compensation. The intercompany services agreement is for services rendered, not for referrals given.

**2.6 How a declined applicant actually arrives:**

Primary path: **applicant submits the Decline-Flow Handoff Intake form** — a single-page form co-branded with the referring bank, hosted in FunnelForge, with routing logic to Consent & Authorization Center. Applicant fills it out themselves; banker's role is to hand off the URL or QR code.

Fallback path: **banker completes a shorter handoff summary** (banker-side; 4 fields: applicant name, applicant email, decline date, one-line context) and Burkham reaches out to the applicant with the intake form URL within 1 business day.

**What does NOT happen:** the banker does NOT send us credit data, financial statements, or the applicant's actual file. That would be an FCRA and consumer-protection landmine (see 2.9). The applicant themselves brings that data through the intake form, with full consent captured in the Consent & Authorization Center.

**2.7 What the declining party tells the applicant about us:**

Three scripts provided in the Handoff Toolkit (short / medium / detailed), all reviewed against Marketing Claim Library. Core message: *"You've been declined for this loan, but there's a firm we work with who can help you get in position for approval in 6–18 months. Their name is Burkham Wickmont. Here's a link — it's up to you whether you want to reach out."*

**Explicit non-scripts:** the banker does NOT say "you'll be approved," "they can guarantee funding," "this will fix your credit," or "you'll get $X." Those would create liability for the bank and would violate our Marketing Claim Library. The Handoff Toolkit training material makes this explicit.

**2.8 Does the applicant know they were referred / do they consent:**

**Yes to both, explicitly and traceably.** The Decline-Flow Handoff Intake form:
- States plainly on the first field that they were referred by [Bank Name] and that their information will be used by Burkham Wickmont for the purposes of assessing capital-architecture services
- Captures explicit consent for information flow (Consent & Authorization Center module 1.5)
- Provides opt-out at every subsequent stage
- Explains the referral relationship (no revenue share; independent firm)
- Includes ECOA / FCRA notices per counsel review `[COMPLIANCE REVIEW PENDING]`

**Applicant is never enrolled without affirmative consent.** No pre-checked boxes. No implied opt-in.

**2.9 What information comes with them:**

**Minimum viable at handoff:** applicant name, applicant email, decline date, one-line context. That's it. Sourced from the banker.

**Everything else comes from the applicant directly**, through the intake flow, with per-item consent. This is intentional structural separation to keep FCRA and adverse-action-notice rules clean:

- **Credit data (FCRA-regulated):** Burkham pulls credit through vendor relationships (business bureau via Nav for Partners / Experian Business API / D&B Direct+; personal credit via Array or equivalent) per Pack Section 1 Decision B. Per-pull client authorization through Consent & Authorization Center. Burkham holds the vendor relationships (not the bank), so FCRA permissible purpose flows through Burkham's engagement with the applicant, not through the bank's referral.
- **Decline reason (adverse-action-notice-regulated):** the bank provides its adverse action notice to the applicant directly, per ECOA §1002.9 and the Bank's own compliance process. Burkham does NOT collect the bank's adverse action notice as part of the handoff. If the applicant chooses to share the notice with Burkham during the Blueprint intake, that is applicant-initiated disclosure, not banker-initiated.

**This separation is intentional.** It keeps Burkham's engagement with the applicant clean of FCRA "furnisher" or "user" complications derived from the bank's original decline. The Blueprint intake creates its own FCRA permissible purpose through the applicant's engagement.

**Even in gated content (Briefing PDFs, founder essays):** TILA advertising rules apply to any consumer-directed communication where a credit product is offered. ## 3.6 Anything about credit — CROA implications

**We do NOT do credit repair. We do NOT market credit repair. We do NOT imply we can improve, repair, or fix credit.**

**Why this matters:** CROA (Credit Repair Organizations Act, 15 USC §1679 et seq.) is one of the strictest consumer-finance statutes on the books. It has:
- Broad definition of "credit repair organization" (any person who uses any instrumentality of interstate commerce to sell or provide any service represented to improve consumer credit record, history, or rating, in exchange for payment)
- Prohibition on payment before services performed
- Written contract requirements
- 3-day cancellation right
- Private right of action
- Actual damages, punitive damages, attorney's fees
- Some states (California, others) have parallel state credit-services-organization statutes that are even stricter

**Burkham operates entirely outside CROA scope by design:**
1. We do not represent that we can improve, repair, or fix a consumer's credit
2. We do not market improvement of consumer credit
3. The Buildout works on **business credit** (D&B Paydex, Experian Business, Equifax Business, LexisNexis SBFE, NAICS optimization, vendor tradeline ladder) — which is not consumer credit and not within CROA scope
4. Personal credit is *monitored* during engagement (credit-profile-preservation commitment) but not *repaired* — the commitment is that our engagement will not damage the personal credit profile, not that we will improve it
5. If a client asks us for credit repair, we decline and refer them out to a licensed credit repair firm (in the states where such licensing exists) — this is documented in the engagement letter

**Marketing Claim Library BANNED list — CROA-triggering phrases:**
- "Improve your credit"
- "Fix your credit"
- "Repair your credit"
- "Boost your credit score"
- "Erase [items] from your credit report"
- "Remove [items] from your credit report"
- "Credit optimization" (borderline — currently banned pending counsel review)
- "Credit enhancement" (borderline — currently banned pending counsel review)
- Any language implying consumer credit outcomes

**Marketing Claim Library APPROVED phrases (credit-adjacent, non-CROA):**
- "Build business credit" (business, not consumer)
- "Structure your business credit profile"
- "Business credit architecture"
- "Preserve your personal credit profile during our engagement" (preservation, not improvement)
- "Credit-profile-preservation commitment"

## 5.7 Where the disqualifier list gets applied

**Not enforced at:** later stages (engagement letter drafting). Getting disqualification wrong at engagement-signing stage is expensive and reputationally bad. The intake-form gate and Blueprint-call gate should catch essentially all disqualifiers.

**Two-way sharing forbidden:** FunnelForge cannot query Console for credit data to segment marketing sequences. Any exception requires founder approval and Marketing Claim Library review of the resulting messaging.

**Referral-fee direction:** **none.** Neither Burkham nor Collingswood nor any other sister venture pays a referral fee to the other. Cross-portfolio referrals are governance-mediated (Gardner) and consent-mediated (client), not fee-mediated. This preserves the arm's-length defense and avoids any regulatory question about referral compensation between related entities.

**Cross-venture services are compensated only for services rendered**, not for referrals given, through the intercompany services agreement (Pack 7.1) `[PENDING outside counsel drafting]`.

## 7.5 Does a customer know Burkham and the other ventures are related

**Yes — disclosed at every engagement.**

**Per-engagement disclosure requirement (Pack 7.2):** conflict-of-interest disclosures auto-generated by Console module 10.1 (Inter-Venture Commerce Hooks), filed to Compliance Evidence Vault (Pack module 7.1), signed by both venture principals.

**Client-facing disclosure text (draft, pending counsel review):**

> *Burkham Wickmont LLC is a subsidiary of Green Companies LLC. Green Companies LLC also owns [list of relevant sister ventures]. [Where applicable:] Your engagement with Burkham Wickmont may involve services rendered to or by these sister ventures at arm's-length pricing under an intercompany services agreement. You are informed of this relationship as part of your engagement. No referral compensation is exchanged between sister ventures. Any cross-venture information sharing requires your explicit consent.*

**When disclosed:** at engagement letter signing (baseline). Also verbally noted during Blueprint call for any client whose situation may involve sister-venture services.

**Not implicitly disclosed:** referrals looking independent when they aren't. If Collingswood refers a client to Burkham, the client is told "Collingswood and Burkham are sister ventures under Green Companies LLC." Full disclosure. No arms-length appearance without arms-length reality.

`[COMPLIANCE REVIEW PENDING]` on the final disclosure language and on the state-by-state disclosure requirements (some states have specific related-party-financial-services disclosure regimes).

# PART 9 — WHAT COULD GO BADLY

## 9.1 Worst thing a marketing worker could do by accident

**Ranked by severity:**

**Category 1 — regulatory-triggering (immediate stop, root-cause, likely reportable):**
1. Send an outbound email containing a banned claim ("guaranteed approval," specific dollar figure, credit repair language, etc.) that was not caught by Communication Compliance Scanner (Pack module 4.2). Even at low volume, a single ship is a Marketing Claim Library breach that can trigger regulator or plaintiff-attorney attention if the recipient forwards it.
2. Send credit data or Plaid data via marketing tier email (mixing marketing and application data — Part 6.3 violation). This would create FCRA exposure.
3. Send outbound communication to a state where Burkham is not licensed to operate.
4. Recharacterize a Burkham service in a way that misrepresents scope (e.g., calling Burkham a lender or broker in writing).
5. Complete a Placement application without per-app written authorization (this is workflow, not marketing — but a worker-generated cover email that implies authorization exists when it doesn't is a marketing-adjacent failure).

**Category 2 — reputationally damaging:**
6. Send a message intended for one segment (say, decline-flow bankers) to a different segment (say, direct-buyer prospects) — audience mismatch. The message doesn't land and may burn credibility with the wrong-audience recipients.
7. Include a specific dollar figure in a founder essay draft that ships (TILA-adjacent trigger even if not classic advertising).
8. Reference a Greenstone or MedLink case study with specific funded-capital figures before those figures are backed and approved (`[ANCHOR DOCS PENDING]`).
9. Fail to include the sister-venture disclosure on outbound copy where the recipient may be a sister-venture client (Pack 7.5).

**Category 3 — operationally costly:**
10. Distribute a Capital Access Briefing to a list that includes unsubscribed contacts (CAN-SPAM exposure).
11. Miss an opt-out request past the 10-day SLA (CAN-SPAM exposure).
12. Ship a template to production with a typo or broken merge tag.

## 9.2 If a worker sent something wrong to 200 people, how would we find out

**Detection paths (ranked by speed):**

1. **Immediate (< 1 hour):** Communication Compliance Scanner (Pack module 4.2) catches the send at the publishing pipeline layer BEFORE it ships. This is the primary defense. Any Banned Claim triggers a pre-send block and routes to Human Approval Console.

2. **Immediate (< 1 hour, backup):** Send-side monitoring. Any outbound blast > N recipients (V1 threshold: 50) requires Human Approval Console explicit approval before send. Worker cannot autonomously blast 200 recipients without a human approving the specific blast.

3. **Within hours:** Recipient reply-based detection. If the send contained an obvious error, some fraction of 200 recipients will reply. Reply routing to Concierge desk surfaces the issue.

4. **Within hours:** Unsubscribe spike detection. FunnelForge monitoring alerts if unsubscribe rate on a send exceeds baseline by threshold.

5. **Within days:** Compliance Review Board weekly agenda review. Even if the send made it through all upstream defenses, weekly Compliance Review Board looks at the week's outbound activity.

6. **Weeks to months:** Bounce-back from external party. Banker complaint, advisor complaint, regulator inquiry. This is the "we found out from the outside" case, and it is the failure mode we most want to avoid.

**Rule per Pack:** ANY outbound send to > 50 recipients requires explicit Human Approval Console approval, no matter how templated. This is a bright-line rule that prevents "worker autonomously blasts 200" scenarios.

## 9.3 Process for pulling a message back

**Cannot un-send an email.** So the response is contain-and-remediate, not retract.

**Step 1 (< 1 hour):** Contain. Freeze the FunnelForge sending capacity for that campaign / template / worker. Prevent further sends of the same content.

**Step 2 (< 4 hours):** Assess scope. How many recipients received the message. Which recipients. What claims were made.

**Step 3 (< 24 hours):** Remediate. Draft correction email to affected recipients (via human — no worker autonomy on correction copy). Correction email:
- Acknowledges the prior message
- States what was incorrect
- States what is accurate
- Provides direct contact for questions
- Does NOT try to re-market or spin

Correction email is reviewed by Ivan or Ira before send.

**Step 4 (< 48 hours):** Root-cause analysis. How did the message get past Communication Compliance Scanner? How did it get past Human Approval Console? Fix the gap. Add to Marketing Claim Library if new banned-claim pattern surfaced. Update templates if template was the issue. Update Authority Levels if worker had excessive autonomy.

**Step 5 (< 5 business days):** Compliance Review Board formal review. Written incident record. Event Ledger entry. Decision on whether to notify counsel, regulator, or affected referrer partners.

**Step 6 (< 30 days):** Follow-up with affected recipients. Was the correction understood? Any downstream complaints? Any bank-partner escalation?

## 9.4 Who gets called when a marketing message causes a complaint

**Immediate response:**
- Village Compliance & Evidence acknowledges receipt of complaint within 1 business day (templated acknowledgment)
- Complaint routes to Human Approval Console for human response

**Complaint response ownership:**

- Complaint from bank partner: Ivan personally (banker relationships are founder-owned in V1)

- Complaints indicating pattern → Marketing Claim Library review + template review + Authority Levels review

## 9.5 Has anything already gone wrong

**Not yet — Burkham is pre-launch.** But the plan is built with awareness of what has gone wrong at *analog* firms:

**Documented patterns from adjacent firms (per 90-day plan and research):**

1. **Fund&Grow-style credit damage complaints.** Firms in the credit-stacking space have accumulated BBB complaint patterns describing customer credit damage from application-velocity violations. Burkham's Issuer Rules Engine + per-app written authorization + credit-profile-preservation commitment are architected specifically to prevent this pattern.

2. **Seek Capital-style class-action exposure.** Firms in the space have faced class-action exposure for percentage-of-credit-limit success fees, marketed dollar amounts not delivered, hidden fees. Burkham's flat success-fee schedule + fee-exhibit-at-signing + 60-day refund trigger are architected specifically to prevent this pattern.

3. **Credit Suite BBB complaint patterns.** Complaints describing deceptive marketing, onboarding delays, refund disputes. Burkham's engagement letter architecture, Deliverable Approval Workflow, and Compliance Evidence Vault audit trail are built to prevent structural analogs.

4. **CFPB enforcement patterns against MCA brokers and adjacent firms.** Burkham's decline-to-do-MCA policy plus refuse-to-broker-MCA policy structurally avoid this surface.

5. **State DFI enforcement patterns (California DFPI, New York DFS).** Burkham's state licensure sequencing (Nevada first, CA/NY/VA priority) plus state-by-state disclosure matrix is architected against this pattern.

6. **CROA class-action patterns against credit repair firms.** Burkham's structural non-engagement in credit repair services plus banned CROA-triggering language in Marketing Claim Library is architected against this pattern.

**Pattern awareness → architecture rule:** every documented adjacent-firm failure mode has a corresponding architectural safeguard in Burkham's design. This is why the compliance architecture is load-bearing, not overhead.

**Standing:** Compliance Review Board weekly agenda (Pack 2.2) includes "state law changes tracked by Regulatory Engine" and "adverse outcomes on any engagement" — so patterns emerging in real time surface early.
